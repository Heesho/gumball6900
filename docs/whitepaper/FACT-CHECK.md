# Whitepaper fact-check register

Edition: whitepaper v0.3, 10 August 2026.

Contracts described: commit `5ad1ebc50f2963c54593430036d384221e0bc10a` (repository head when this
edition was prepared). Internal audit register candidate: `54e3f2c3ce1de25aea4da2f21fab27804a3bfa84`,
baseline `395a0dfbf56e3d478233736ef7a110e584a676e7`. Production Solidity changed between the register
candidate and the described head (checked increments in `Fundraiser.settleEpochs`; the ADR 0022
LiquidityPosition redesign); the finalized internal campaign recorded in
`packages/contracts/audit/TEST-CAMPAIGN.md` covers the post-ADR-0022 tree committed as `5ad1ebc`.

Source-of-truth order used throughout: production Solidity under `packages/contracts/src/core`,
then executable tests, generated ABIs, independent TypeScript/Python models, accepted ADRs, audit
records, SDK, subgraph, frontend, and finally prose documents. Where sources disagreed, the
production Solidity won and the disagreement is recorded in this file.

Verification-status legend:

- **Verified-onchain** - the claim is enforced by production contract code and covered by tests.
- **Verified-model** - the claim is arithmetic reproduced independently (TypeScript and Python) and
  matched against the committed simulation fixture; the whitepaper build re-derives it and fails on
  mismatch (`docs/whitepaper/src/protocol-facts.mjs`).
- **Verified-record** - the claim restates a recorded engineering result (test counts, gas, coverage)
  from `packages/contracts/audit/`; it is internal evidence, not independent assurance.
- **Disclosed-limitation** - the claim is a stated risk or open finding, not a guarantee.

Every "Enforced" column answers: is the claim enforced by onchain code (rather than by convention,
frontend, or documentation)? All claims assume the supported-token model (standard, non-rebasing,
exact-transfer ERC-20 behavior; see `docs/SUPPORTED-TOKEN-MODEL.md`) unless stated otherwise.
